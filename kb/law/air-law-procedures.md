#### Key definitions — CAR 101.01
<!-- id: law-car-101-01-key-definitions | level: PPL CPL IR -->

| Term | CAR 101.01 meaning (short) |
|---|---|
| controlled airspace | fixed dimensions in DAH; ATC service provided |
| IMC / VMC | below / at-or-above Div VI VMC minima (vis + cloud distance) |
| flight visibility | forward visibility from cockpit in flight |
| RVR | range pilot on centreline can expect to see rwy markings / lights |
| MDA | ASL, non-precision; no descent below until required visual reference |
| DH | height (CAP/RCAP) for precision approach: missed if no visual ref |
| night | end of evening civil twilight → start of morning civil twilight |
| transponder | Mode S ATC transponder (CAN-TSO-C112) |

- `T:` MDA = altitude (ASL); DH = height. CAP: DA = altitude, DH = DA above TDZE/threshold.
- `T:` IMC is defined by VMC minima, not by "in cloud".

#### Entry, transit and departure of aircraft — AIP Canada GEN 1.2
<!-- id: law-entry-transit-departure-gen-1-2 | level: CPL -->

- `R` International ARR/DEP → **first land at an A/D with customs** (CFS/CWAS heading `CUST` = airport of entry, AOE).
- `R` Foreign commercial: Canadian **Foreign Air Operator Certificate (FAOC)**; scheduled also needs designation + Agency scheduled licence; non-scheduled needs prior Agency permission + licence. Overflight without FAOC → request **≥10 working days** ahead.
- `R` **Private flights** into or over Canada: no prior permission, but **flight plan** required. Customs decides private vs commercial by how the flight actually operates.
- `R` Transborder: flight plan **required** for every flight Canada ↔ foreign state (602.73). Canada → U.S.: eAPIS manifest **≥60 min before DEP**. U.S. → Canada: land at CBSA-designated A/D; **≤15 persons** may report by telephone **2–48 h** before ARR + call on landing; >15 → staffed AOE. CANPASS (all on board members, ≤15) → any AOE when open.

- Sources: AIP Canada GEN 1.2 (NAV CANADA, edition of 2 Oct 2025).

#### Canada Transportation Act — air service licence
<!-- id: law-canada-transportation-act-air-service-licence | level: CPL -->

- `R` CTA s. 57: no one operates an **air service** without, for that service: a **licence** under CTA Part II · a **Canadian aviation document** (normally an AOC) · prescribed **liability insurance**. s. 59: no selling or advertising an air service without the required licence.
- `R` Air service = aircraft service **publicly available** for carrying passengers and/or goods. Licences: **domestic** · **scheduled international** · **non-scheduled international**, issued by the **Canadian Transportation Agency**.
- `T` AOC (TC, safety) ≠ licence (Agency, economic): an air service needs both, plus insurance.
- `?:` Air Transportation Regulations Parts I and VII not read for this note.

- Sources: [CTA s. 57](https://lois-laws.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-10.4/page-9.html); [Agency licence guide](https://otc-cta.gc.ca/eng/publication/canadian-air-carrier-licence-application-guide).

#### Canada Labour Code Part II on board aircraft
<!-- id: law-canada-labour-code-on-board-ohs | level: CPL -->

- `R` Part II (occupational health and safety) covers employees **on board aircraft in operation**: flying, or doors closed and moving under own power for TKOF/LDG. Not when stationary before, after or between flights. TC administers it (MOU with ESDC Labour Program) with the Aviation OHS Regulations.
- `R` Employee rights: **know** hazards · **participate** (committee / representative) · **refuse dangerous work** (s. 128). Refusing on board: tell the **person in charge of the aircraft**; health and safety officer investigates. No refusal if it directly endangers another person's life, health or safety.
- `R` Employer duties (ss. 124–125) protect employees' health and safety; employee duties (s. 126). Death or disabling injury to ≥2 employees → employer contacts TC OHS inspector **within 24 h**.
- `?:` Sections 124–126 summarized from TC brochures, not read in the Code.

- Sources: [TC TP 14215](https://tc.canada.ca/node/29631); [TC TP 13537](https://tc.canada.ca/en/aviation/publications/right-refuse-dangerous-work-board-aircraft-while-operation-tp-13537); [TC TP 14793](https://tc.canada.ca/sites/default/files/2024-07/TP_14793_E.pdf).

#### Dangerous goods by air — TC AIM RAC 12.3
<!-- id: law-dangerous-goods-by-air-rac-12-3 | level: CPL -->

- `R` Dangerous goods = items in the **9 classes** of the TDG Act: explosives · gases · flammable liquids · flammable solids · oxidizers · toxic/infectious · radioactive · corrosives · miscellaneous.
- `R` Not carried on Canadian aircraft, or foreign aircraft in Canada, unless per **TDG Act + Regulations**; TDGR 12.1–12.3 adopt the **ICAO Technical Instructions**; 12.4–12.17 give domestic alternatives.
- `R` Anyone handling, offering or transporting DG: valid **TDG training certificate** (Part 6) or direct supervision by a certified person. Certificate valid **24 months**.
- `R` Air operators submit DG procedures + training to TC; may delegate tasks but stay accountable. 604 private operators may not submit, but ICAO TIs still require a training program even if they never carry DG.

- Sources: [TC AIM RAC 12.3](https://tc.canada.ca/sites/default/files/2026-10/aim-2026-2_rac_en.pdf).
